Governance with Discipline to Drive AI Success

Multi-layered governance framework ensuring ethical, compliant, and value-driven AI delivery

Core Governance Principle

Governance that is "seamless for business to adopt" - not burdensome
Right level of governance with right coverage to manage risks while enabling speed of delivery

Ethical Steering Committee

Chair: UK&I Chief UW Officer (SMF23)
  • Approve guiding principles for ethical AI use
  • Review/challenge AI initiatives for customer outcomes
  • Final approval authority for high-impact AI
  • Six-monthly reporting cadence
  • FCA/GDPR/EU AI Act compliance oversight

Ethical Working Group

Chair: Chief Technical UW Officer
  • Review AI initiatives at inception
  • Mandatory DPIA sign-off before production
  • Business rules and ethical impact assessment
  • Monthly meeting cadence
  • Escalate high-risk cases to SteerCo
  • Current Gap: Only 2 of 48 initiatives have completed DPIA

Program Steering Committee

Program-Level Governance
  • Strategic programs own AI use cases (CL Tech & Workflow, CL Pass, Guidewire, One Commercial, Symphony)
  • Monthly steering committee meetings
  • Quarterly gate reviews for major milestones
  • Budget and resource allocation decisions
  • Cross-program dependency management

Change Control Board

Technical & Operational Governance
  • Evaluate, approve, and track changes to AI systems
  • Model version control and retraining governance
  • Integration testing and deployment approval
  • Post-deployment performance monitoring
  • Incident response and remediation oversight

Governance Workflow for AI Initiatives

Stage Governance Body Required Actions
Ideation Business Product Owner + CIO Initial use case submission • Alignment to strategic priorities • Preliminary feasibility assessment
Evaluation Ethical Working Group DPIA initiation • Ethical impact assessment • 10-lever scoring validation • Data privacy review
Prioritization Program Steering Committee Portfolio placement decision • Resource allocation approval • Budget confirmation • Dependency mapping

Deploy Ethical Working Group Production readiness review • Final DPIA sign-off • Deployment authorization • Monitoring plan approval

Key Risks & Mitigations for AI Initiatives

AI-specific risks identified in RSA AI Maturity Assessment with targeted mitigation strategies

Risk Category Mitigation Measures
Regulatory Compliance
(FCA, GDPR, EU AI Act)		 • Establish framework to identify regulatory change and manage compliance proactively
• Mandatory DPIA completion for all AI initiatives before production
• Ethical SteerCo and Working Group review aligned to FCA/GDPR requirements
• Quarterly compliance assessments and regulatory horizon scanning
• Distinct UK/EU compliance requirements (not Intact Canada framework)
Model Risk & Accuracy
(Drift, bias, explainability)		 • Implement pre-implementation and ongoing model testing frameworks
• Increased supervision with "human in the loop" for high-stakes decisions
• Use training models to recognize normal/abnormal patterns and generate alerting
• Address current MLOps maturity gaps (model retraining, drift monitoring)
• Establish model performance tracking and validation cadence
Data Quality & Privacy
(PII, accuracy, completeness)		 • Implement Dynamic Data Obfuscation & Redaction controls to reduce data loss risk
• Implement feedback loops and remediation to manage data accuracy and bias
• Connect AI initiatives to Enterprise Data Model (EDM) and EDP roadmap
• Address fragmented data landscape (8 policy admin systems, 2 CRMs)
• Enforce data privacy, access controls, and retention policies through IT/Data Governance
SME Availability
(Domain expert bandwidth)		 • Publish stakeholder engagement plan and prioritize use cases based on SME availability
• Leverage AI-powered accelerators to minimize SME time requirements
• Secure timely access to SMEs for validations and critical decisions
• Two-in-a-box model: BA and tester work together to validate insurance-specific logic
• Focus on insurance validation points: capital evolution, risk rating, premium calculation
Technical Complexity & Debt
(Integration, legacy systems)		 • Build co-existence strategies for legacy and modernized systems during transition
• Adopt loosely coupled architecture design to minimize integration impact
• Avoid tactical "Unicorn" solutions - align to Architecture Blueprint strategic platforms
• Don't increase reliance on systems to be decommissioned (legacy by 2028 target)
• Embed AI in DevOps with CI/CD pipelines and automated testing
Adoption & Change Management
(User resistance, training)		 • Leverage OCM function to ensure organizational preparedness and plan necessary skilling
• Prepare business users with role-based training (agents, brokers, adjusters, underwriters)
• Drive adoption through workshops, pilot rollouts, and structured feedback loops
• Broadcast and celebrate milestones to boost morale and engagement
• Front-line adoption by distributed users is critical - get early feedback to reduce post-launch changes
Cost Overruns & ROI
(LLM costs, benefit realization)		 • Onboard models into FinOps process; review and adapt for Gen AI cost models
• Identify charging model for AI tools and agree tooling as part of AI roadmap
• Learn from initial use cases to improve cost estimation capability (review PoCs for accuracy)
• Right-size model selection and licensing to minimize duplication of capabilities
• Track actual ROI against projections and feed back to Value Framework
Talent & Skills Gap
(AI expertise retention)		 • Define sourcing strategy for AI talent (build vs. buy vs. partner)
• Agree standards for documentation and knowledge transfer to build institutional capability
• Invest in automation to reduce dependency on scarce skills
• Consider AI upskilling and awareness programs across the organization
• Only host models in-house where necessary to manage resource/skill requirements
SOURCES: RSA AI Maturity Assessment (AI-specific risks section), Hexaware workshop risk identification, Architecture Blueprint pain points, IT Pain Points Workshop, Data Team feedback on MLOps maturity, Stakeholder interviews on current-state challenges.

Best Practices for AI Delivery at Scale

Proven practices from RSA's established delivery model and industry leaders adapted for AI transformation

Learning-Led Approach

  • Weight learning value over immediate financial benefit in early steps
  • Challenge business thinking and expand AI horizons
  • Build cross-functional design and delivery muscles
  • Diverse technical learning across integration patterns
  • Test governance and operating model MVP
  • Validate AI-to-value linkage for stakeholder credibility

Leverage COE Model

  • CIO organization pioneers technology introduction
  • Pilot with business partners (not IT working in isolation)
  • Deliver common AI capabilities at scale
  • Hub-and-spoke for Data & Analytics (federated scientists)
  • Platform teams provide reusable integration patterns
  • Cross-functional pods with P&L accountability

Governance by Design

  • Embed governance from inception (not afterthought)
  • DPIA as mandatory regulatory gate
  • FCA/GDPR/EU AI Act compliance from Day 1
  • Governance that is seamless for business to adopt
  • Risk assessment at appropriate level (use case vs. capability)
  • Route through Ethical Working Group → SteerCo

Enterprise Integration

  • Connect to Enterprise Data Model (EDM) and EDP roadmap
  • Leverage strategic platforms: Power UP, Instabase, Radar Live, Guidewire
  • Avoid tactical "Unicorn" solutions and technical debt
  • Don't increase reliance on systems to be decommissioned
  • Move from tactical data marts to enterprise architecture
  • Address system fragmentation through consolidation

Agile Delivery Cadence

  • Deliver in demonstrable 8-12 week increments
  • 2-week Agile sprints with clear success criteria
  • Monthly steering committee + quarterly gate reviews
  • Continuous sprint demos and stakeholder feedback
  • Plan for dress rehearsal before production cutover
  • Hypercare support post-deployment

Define Clear Acceptance Criteria

  • Upfront establish acceptance and exit criteria for each phase
  • Align with business objectives per RACI matrix
  • Two-in-a-box validation (BA + tester for insurance logic)
  • Focus on insurance-specific validation points
  • UAT with front-line users before enterprise rollout
  • Track actual outcomes vs. projections

Plan SME Engagement

  • Publish SME engagement plan early
  • Secure timely access for critical decisions and validations
  • Prioritize workstreams based on SME availability
  • Leverage AI accelerators to minimize SME time
  • Domain SMEs define how system behaves for each LOB
  • Early involvement prevents late-stage rework

Invest in Test Environments

  • Execute PoC/PoT to validate strategies and establish confidence
  • Invest in test automation to ensure comprehensive coverage
  • Establish pre-prod that mimics production for NFR validation
  • Ensure adequate capacity for parallel workstreams
  • Address current gaps in automated validation and testing
  • Build robust Guidewire AI pipeline (currently not mature)

Comprehensive Documentation

  • Business rules, functional specs, solution architecture
  • Test strategy, test cases, test data, automation scripts
  • Model documentation (algorithms, training data, metrics)
  • Integration patterns and reusable components catalogued
  • Knowledge transfer sessions for organizational learning
  • Consider AI upskilling programs across organization

One Commercial Guiding Principle (Applied to AI)

"Speed is critical. One value proposition allows us to simplify processes, mix teams and eliminate 'RSA way, NIG way' conversations and move onto the 'new way'"
Applied to AI: Eliminate siloed approaches, create unified AI delivery model, accelerate through standardization, enable "One AI Way" across RSA.

Clarifying RSA Stakeholder Roles in AI Delivery Success

Insurance-specific roles and responsibilities for AI initiatives - what RSA stakeholders own vs. delivery partner responsibilities

Business Product Owner (e.g., Head of Claims, UW, or Policy Admin)

RESPONSIBILITIES:
• Prioritizes features based on business value
• Validates user stories and functional workflows
• Acts as bridge between IT and operations
• Defines success criteria and acceptance criteria
• Final sign-off on UAT and production readiness
WHY CRITICAL:
Anchors the AI solution in real insurance business processes (e.g., FNOL, premium calculation, endorsements, claims triage). Without PO ownership, AI becomes technology looking for a problem.

Domain SMEs (Claims Analysts, Underwriters, Actuaries)

RESPONSIBILITIES:
• Provide business rules for rating, reserving, policy issuance, claims triage
• Verification of business rules and workflow processes
• Participate in gap analysis and UAT
• Validate model outputs for accuracy and reasonableness
WHY CRITICAL:
These experts define how the AI system should behave for each Line of Business (LOB). AI models trained without SME validation risk incorrect business logic, poor risk selection, and regulatory non-compliance.

CIO Organization (Platform Teams) - COE Model

RESPONSIBILITIES:
• Pioneer technology introduction and pilot with business
• Deliver common AI capabilities (Copilot, Instabase, Power Platform)
• Provide technical architecture and integration patterns
• Ensure alignment to Architecture Blueprint and strategic platforms
WHY CRITICAL:
RSA's established COE model for tech delivery at scale. CIO pioneers then pilots with business partners - not working in isolation. Critical to avoid tactical solutions and technical debt.

Data & Analytics Team (Hub-and-Spoke Model)

RESPONSIBILITIES:
• EDP integration, data quality frameworks, analytics infrastructure
• Data scientists embedded in business teams (Claims, Pricing, UW)
• ML model development, validation, and monitoring
• Connect AI initiatives to Enterprise Data Model (EDM)
WHY CRITICAL:
Central platform + federated BI/MI ensures data foundation for AI. Hub-and-spoke model embeds data scientists in business context while maintaining enterprise architecture standards.

Enterprise Architecture (Graham Wilde team)

RESPONSIBILITIES:
• Solution architecture review and integration pattern validation
• Alignment to Architecture Blueprint and strategic platforms
• Avoid tactical "Unicorn" solutions and technical debt
• Ensure AI integrates with modernization roadmap (decommission legacy by 2028)
WHY CRITICAL:
Prevents AI initiatives from creating technical debt or increasing reliance on systems to be decommissioned. Ensures diverse technical learning across integration patterns.

Ethical SteerCo + Working Group (Andrew Tong governance)

RESPONSIBILITIES:
• Approve guiding principles for ethical AI use
• Review/challenge AI initiatives for customer outcomes and regulatory compliance
• Mandatory DPIA sign-off before production deployment
• FCA/GDPR/EU AI Act compliance embedded from inception
WHY CRITICAL:
Insurance is highly regulated. Governance-by-design ensures compliance, ethical use, and avoids reputational/regulatory risk. DPIA is regulatory gate - non-negotiable.

IT & Data Governance Leads

RESPONSIBILITIES:
• Integration with core systems (PAS, CRM, Guidewire, EDP)
• Infrastructure readiness (cloud/on-prem deployment)
• Data privacy, access controls, retention policies
• Security tooling to support AI processes (Dynamic Data Obfuscation, PII controls)
WHY CRITICAL:
Insurance deals with legacy tech, complex integrations, and regulated data (PII). Governance ensures data security, privacy compliance, and operational resilience.

Change Management & Training

RESPONSIBILITIES:
• Prepare business users for new AI-powered tools and workflows
• Create role-based training (agents, brokers, adjusters, underwriters, claims handlers)
• Drive adoption through workshops, feedback loops, and pilot rollouts
• Organizational Change Management (OCM) for cultural transformation
WHY CRITICAL:
Front-line adoption by distributed users (brokers, agents, adjusters) is critical to AI success. Success depends on thousands of users embracing new ways of working - especially in broker-led channels.

End Users (Agents, Brokers, Claims Adjusters, Underwriters)

RESPONSIBILITIES:
• Participate in design validation, usability testing, and pilot rollouts
• Report pain points and suggest improvements during UAT
• Provide feedback on AI outputs and model accuracy
• Champion adoption within their teams
WHY CRITICAL:
They are the system's primary users - getting their feedback early ensures better adoption and fewer change requests post go-live. Front-line input prevents building AI that doesn't meet real-world needs.

Compliance & Risk Officers

RESPONSIBILITIES:
• Review AI initiatives for regulatory compliance (FCA, GDPR, EU AI Act)
• Approve use cases, user stories, and functional workflows from compliance lens
• Ensure model explainability and audit trails
• Validate that AI doesn't create discrimination or unfair outcomes
WHY CRITICAL:
Insurance is highly regulated, and compliance must be built into the AI lifecycle. Prevents regulatory exposure, reputational damage, and ensures consumer protection.

Critical Success Factor

Multi-party collaboration is essential for AI delivery success. RSA stakeholders own business context, domain expertise, governance, and change management. Delivery partners provide technical execution, but cannot succeed without active RSA engagement across all these roles. "AI is at the top of our list...from overall organization perspective" - this requires whole-organization commitment, not just IT project execution.
